Draw this as one standalone SVG.

Subject: A hypothetical city block on a quiet South Florida street: one focus building on its own lot, two or three neighboring buildings, a few round-canopy trees, and a frontage road along the front edge. The focus building is the single point of emphasis.

Style: a leaded stained-glass window of the scene, seen straight on from inside, with daylight coming through it. The window is a wide segmental arch (straight sides, a shallow arched top) set in a dark wall that fills the rest of the frame. Inside: a pale sky broken into large irregular facets that fan out from an amber sun in the upper right; neighbour buildings in cobalt; the focus building in ruby, the only red, with small amber window panes; round tree canopies in emerald, each canopy split into three or four wedge panes; a band of emerald lawn; the frontage road along the bottom in deep cobalt with an amber centre line. Every pane is separated from its neighbours by black lead came. Cames vary in width the way real leading does: the window border is heaviest, the outlines of the main shapes are medium, the internal subdivisions of a shape are thin. Adjacent panes of the same colour must differ slightly in tone, as cut glass from different sheets does.

Palette (CSS custom properties in a `<style>` block on `svg`, used with `var()`): `--pale: #f4ead0` (clear cathedral glass), `--amber: #eeb23a`, `--ruby: #b5233a`, `--cobalt: #2452a6`, `--emerald: #1f8a5b`, `--lead: #1b1a1f`.

SVG mechanics:
1. One geometry, two passes. Define every pane once inside `<defs><g id="panes">`. Each pane path gets its colour and its came weight from inherited custom properties only: in CSS, `#panes path{fill:var(--glass-on, var(--c)); stroke:var(--came, none); stroke-width:var(--w, 2.5); stroke-linejoin:round}`, with per-pane `style="--c:var(--ruby)"` and `style="--w:5"` on main outlines. Then render `<use href="#panes"/>` once as glass (no overrides), and again as `<use href="#panes" style="--glass-on:none; --came:var(--lead)"/>` for the lead. Custom properties inherit into the `<use>` shadow tree, so the second pass is the came network of exactly the same edges. Paint shapes in back-to-front order (sky facets, buildings, trees, lawn, road) so later panes cover earlier ones; the lead pass then outlines every visible edge.
2. Tone variation: give same-colour neighbouring panes slightly different fills by setting their `--c` to `color-mix(in srgb, var(--cobalt) 82%, white)` or `color-mix(in srgb, var(--cobalt) 85%, black)` (and likewise for the other colours), never a new hue. Roughly a third lighter, a third darker, a third plain.
3. Glass texture: on the glass pass, a filter that adds `feTurbulence type="fractalNoise" baseFrequency="0.035 0.05" numOctaves="3"` → `feColorMatrix` to a grey ramp → `feComposite operator="arithmetic"` with `SourceGraphic` (k1≈0.55, k2≈0.5) for a soft mottle, then `feGaussianBlur stdDeviation="4"` of the result blended back with `feBlend mode="screen"` at low strength so light bleeds a little over the edges (halation).
4. Light through the glass: over the glass pass and under the lead, a full-window `radialGradient` centred on the sun (white at 0 → amber at 25% opacity 0.5 → transparent at 70%) with `mix-blend-mode:screen`, so the sky near the sun glows and the far corners stay saturated and darker.
5. Rounded lead: on the lead pass, a filter that blurs `SourceAlpha` (`stdDeviation="1.2"`), lights it with `feSpecularLighting surfaceScale="3" specularConstant="0.9" specularExponent="18" lighting-color="#fff"` and `feDistantLight azimuth="235" elevation="40"`, composites it `in` `SourceAlpha`, and adds it to the lead with `feComposite operator="arithmetic" k2="1" k3="0.5"`: dull grey-black came with a faint highlight running along each strip.
6. Clip both passes to the arch with a `<clipPath>`; draw the wall in `--lead` with a slightly lighter inner reveal, and the arch border as the heaviest came (`stroke-width` about 9).

Output rules: `viewBox="0 0 560 336"`, `xmlns` set, no width/height attributes, under 60 KB, no raster images, no external references, no scripts, no animation, no text.
