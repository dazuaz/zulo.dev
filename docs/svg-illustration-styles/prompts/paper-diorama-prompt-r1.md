Draw this as one standalone SVG.

Subject: A hypothetical city block on a quiet South Florida street: one focus building on its own lot, two or three neighboring buildings, a few round-canopy trees, and a frontage road along the front edge. The focus building is the single point of emphasis.

Style: a layered cut-paper diorama seen straight on, like a shadow box. Six flat sheets of tinted paper stacked from back to front: (1) sky, (2) a low far band of distant palms and roofs, (3) the neighboring buildings and round-canopy trees, (4) the focus building and its lot, (5) the frontage road with a curb line, (6) a foreground strip of grass and a hedge cut with a wavy top edge that overlaps the bottom of the road. Every sheet is a single flat colour with crisp, slightly irregular cut edges; no gradients on the paper itself, no outlines. Depth comes only from shadow. The focus building is the one warm coral sheet in an otherwise cool, quiet set, sits a little forward of its neighbours, and its windows are cut-through holes.

Palette (CSS custom properties in a `<style>` block on `svg`, used with `var()`): `--sky: #f2e6d4` (cream), `--far: #bcd7cc` (pale mint), `--mid: #79ab9f` (sea green), `--sand: #e3c7a1` (neighbour paper), `--focus: #e2735a` (coral), `--ink: #3a2e3b` (plum, used for shadows and the road). Shades of these come only from shadow and texture, never new hues.

SVG mechanics:
1. One `<filter id="paper">` used by every sheet, built in this order: `feTurbulence type="fractalNoise" baseFrequency="0.8" numOctaves="3"` → `feDiffuseLighting surfaceScale="1.2" lighting-color="#fff"` with `feDistantLight azimuth="225" elevation="65"` (a fibre bump map) → `feComposite operator="arithmetic"` multiplying it onto `SourceGraphic` (k1≈0.9, k3≈0.12) → `feComposite operator="in" in2="SourceAlpha"` so the grain stays inside the sheet. Then the shadow: two shadows from `SourceAlpha`, a tight contact shadow (`feGaussianBlur stdDeviation="1.2"`, `feOffset dx="1.5" dy="2"`, 45% opacity) and a soft ambient one (`stdDeviation="5"`, `dx="4" dy="6"`, 25% opacity), each tinted with `feFlood flood-color="#3a2e3b"` + `feComposite operator="in"`. `feMerge`: ambient, contact, textured sheet. Give the filter region generous padding (x/y −10%, width/height 125%) so shadows are not clipped.
2. Light comes from the upper left, so every shadow falls down and to the right onto the sheet directly behind. Keep the offset identical for all sheets: equal spacing between sheets.
3. Each sheet is its own `<g filter="url(#paper)">`, painted back to front. Group all shapes that belong to one sheet together (for example all neighbour buildings and trees in sheet 3) so the sheet casts one combined shadow.
4. Cut windows and doors as real holes with a `<mask>` (white sheet, black window rects) on an inner `<g mask>`, with the filter on the outer `<g>`. Because the shadow is built from the masked alpha, each window shows the sheet behind with a small shadow along its top and left inner edge — that is what sells the cut.
5. Draw the sky sheet edge to edge without a shadow; give the whole box a thin inner frame shadow (a full-size rect with `feGaussianBlur` on an inverted inset) so it reads as a shadow box.
6. Round-canopy trees are circles or slightly lumpy blobs on short trunks, part of their sheet's single path group. Keep shape counts low; the craft is in the layering, not detail.

Output rules: `viewBox="0 0 560 336"`, `xmlns` set, no width/height attributes, under 60 KB, no raster images, no external references, no scripts, no animation, no text.
