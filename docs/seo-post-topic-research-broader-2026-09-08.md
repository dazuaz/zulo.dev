## Recommended next post

**Can AI-written text be watermarked? What a watermark actually proves**

This is my strongest combination of a recent development, broad personal relevance, and concrete search questions. It touches anyone who writes, edits, translates, teaches, or reads online. The editorial argument: evidence that an AI participated in a document does not resolve how the document was authored.

The immediate hook is Anthropic's August 14 explanation of Claude text watermarking, updated September 1 with detection API information. It describes statistical marking without hidden characters, distinguishes watermark checks from ordinary AI detectors, and says a mark cannot separate generation from heavy editing. Detection access is a private preview. [Anthropic's explanation](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-text-watermark).

Search already surfaces news stories, explainers, and commercial detector pages. This is competitive, not an untouched keyword. The useful angle is a precise, dated guide to evidence and interpretation, including changes since launch coverage. For example, the September 1 API update makes earlier claims that no detector exists incomplete. Do not imply every historical model or output is marked.

Candidate queries: `AI text watermarking`, `does Claude watermark text`, `AI watermark vs AI detector`. Suggested slug: `/blog/ai-text-watermarking`.

A future article could explain marking with a small original toy example, compare the questions answered by watermarking, detection, and file provenance, then consider writing that mixes human and AI contributions. Label a toy example as illustrative; do not present it as a working Claude detector. Consult the underlying [SynthID research](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-08025-4) before developing the technical explanation; this paper was linked by Anthropic but was not read during this topic-selection pass. Keep legal interpretation outside the premise unless researched separately.
